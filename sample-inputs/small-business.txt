We own a neighbourhood cafe with eight tables and a short menu. Last spring we were sure we needed to add more items to bring in new customers, so we spent weeks planning a bigger menu with sandwiches, salads and three new desserts.

Before we printed anything, a regular told us she had almost stopped coming because of the wait at the till on weekday mornings. That surprised us, so we asked five other regulars the same question: what almost made you go somewhere else this month? Four of them mentioned the queue. Nobody mentioned the menu.

So we dropped the big menu plan and fixed the small things first. We timed one busy hour and counted the steps for each order, and we cut two of them. We put a small sign at the door listing anything sold out, so people no longer got a surprise at the counter. We moved the card reader next to the milk fridge so the barista does not walk back and forth.

The queue is shorter now and our regulars have said so. We still have the short menu. The lesson for us was to ask the people already in the room before spending money on something new, and to fix friction before adding choice.

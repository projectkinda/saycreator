Over the past year our small design agency noticed that most of our revision rounds came from the same cause: unclear briefs. When a client says they will know it when they see it, that usually means the goal is not decided yet, and we end up designing three versions of a guess.

We now ask for the same things before any work begins. First, one sentence describing what success looks like three months after launch; if the client cannot write it, the brief is not ready. Second, one named decision maker, because feedback from five people is really five briefs. Third, a budget range. Fourth, a real deadline and the reason for it. Fifth, two examples the client likes and why.

We put all of this on a single page and ask the client to sign off on it. If the page needs to be longer than one page, we treat that as a sign the goal is still too vague and we pause to decide it together.

The change has been clear to our team. Projects start faster, the first draft is closer to what the client wanted, and we spend less time in the middle of a project arguing about what was meant. We share the one-page template with every new client now and we ask them to fill it in on a call with us, not alone.

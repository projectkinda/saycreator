I run a small YouTube channel about woodworking and for a long time I filmed one video a day. It was exhausting. Every morning I had to pick a topic, set up the lights, film, and then edit at night. I never had a day off and my videos kept getting worse because I was rushing.

Last year I changed one thing: I now film five videos in one afternoon, once a week. The night before, I write down the five titles and a rough outline for each. I use the same setup for all five, same light, same shirt, same background, and I only change the project on the bench. The next day I edit all five back to back while the outlines are still fresh in my head.

Three things made it work. First, deciding the titles before filming, because deciding while the camera is on is what used to eat my hours. Second, keeping one setup so I never rebuild the lights. Third, keeping filming and editing as two separate blocks of time and never mixing them.

Some people told me batching would make the videos feel stale. It did not. Viewers only see the finished video, and posting on time matters more to them than whether I filmed it on the same day. I now have two full days off every week and I have not missed an upload since I started.
